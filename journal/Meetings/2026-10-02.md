## What we discussed / decided

After the meeting on the 24th, we dug deeper into the USENIX 2026 paper and existing anti-cheat literature to evaluate our three research directions. We had a WhatsApp discussion and quick phone sync to weigh pros/cons and think about what each Report would actually argue.

**Current thinking: All three options have merit. We're exploring which one makes the strongest XAI research contribution as per in person chat with James.**

We reviewed each option's research angle:
- **Option A** (Visual Detection via YOLO): Explainability of image-based aimbot detection
- **Option B** (False Positive Analysis): Explaining why behavioral detection flags legitimate players
- **Option C** (Adversarial Robustness): Using explainability to defend anti-cheat systems against sophisticated attacks

## Why (trade-offs we're weighing)

Each option has different research angles and trade-offs:

**Option A — Visual Detection (YOLO + Explainability):**
- Strength: Concrete, visually intuitive for stakeholders. Real aimbot detection problem.
- Weakness: Labeled training data is hard to obtain. Can't easily get real aimbot footage for ground truth. Would rely on synthetic cheating, which may not generalize to real exploits.
- XAI alignment: Lower—we'd be stretching Week 3–4 concepts (SHAP on tabular data) into vision domain.

**Option B — False Positive Analysis (Behavioral Anomaly Detection):**
- Strength: Directly applies SHAP and TreeExplainer to tabular data (our course focus). Addresses a real stakeholder problem: why was I wrongly flagged? Clear connection to player trust.
- Weakness: Behavioral-only detection won't catch visual aimbots—our analysis would miss a major threat vector. Requires us to defend this limitation in the Report.
- XAI alignment: Highest—directly maps to SHAP methodology and Week 3–4 techniques.

**Option C — Adversarial Robustness:**
- Strength: Novel research angle. Combines adversarial ML with XAI—frontier stuff.
- Weakness: Scope creep risk. Adversarial robustness metrics (robustness, transferability, etc.) aren't in course scope. Could become a PhD thesis instead of a 6-week project.
- XAI alignment: Medium—interesting but tangential to core SHAP/TreeExplainer concepts.

We're leaning toward Option B for course alignment and tractability, but we want to explore all three before finalising.

## Decision Rationale

We're evaluating each option by asking: **Which gives us the strongest XAI research that we are interested in. We are also looking for the strongest topic for a presentation and short-paper**

**Criteria and Comparison:**

1. **Course alignment vs. Real-world completeness**
   - Option A (visual): More complete threat picture (catches visual aimbots), but forces us outside SHAP/TreeExplainer comfort zone.
   - Option B (behavioral): Cleanest use of course concepts (SHAP on tabular data), but deliberately ignores a major attack vector.
   - Option C (adversarial): Novel blend, but risks scope creep into adversarial ML (not in course).

2. **Stakeholder clarity**
   - Option A: "Here's why the detector saw an aimbot" (technical, impersonal).
   - Option B: "Here's why we thought you were cheating" (personal, player-centric, connects to trust).
   - Option C: "Here's how we defend against adaptive cheaters" (defensive, meta-level).

3. **Measurable contribution**
   - Option A: Saliency visualization + YOLO robustness analysis.
   - Option B: SHAP-based false-positive explanation framework + limitations discussion.
   - Option C: Adversarial SHAP robustness evaluation (novel but risky).
