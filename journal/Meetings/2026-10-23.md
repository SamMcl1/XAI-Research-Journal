## What we discussed / decided
We met to go over draft 1 of the paper and finish it for submission. We worked through the open questions from our 18 October meeting and agreed on the final changes:

- Rewrote the abstract so it states our findings more clearly.
- Changed our main argument. In draft 1 we said the per-tick explanations held all the useful information. After going back over it, we realised they explain the window model, not the final kill score. So neither level of XGuardian's explanation actually breaks down the verdict. They show a reviewer where to look, but not why the player was flagged.
- Simplified the false positive example so it's easier to follow. The result is the same: roughly 46% of flags would be innocent players.
- Fixed the limitations. We did look at XGuardian's code but couldn't run it because it needs older dependencies.
- Added some background on GDPR and LIME, and checked and fixed all our references and figures.
- Updated the README with our final topic, scope and a link to the paper.

## Why
Some of the claims in draft 1 were stronger than our evidence supported. Fixing the per-tick point made our argument more accurate and actually stronger, since it shows the explanations fall short at both levels, not just one.

## Open questions / unresolved
None. All the open questions from 18 October have been resolved, and the paper is ready to submit.
